After a refreshing two-month break from riding, the call of the open road during the monsoon was too strong to resist. A few fellow Triumph riders and I decided on a one-day ride to Madhe Ghat Waterfall, drawn by the promise of cascading water, misty mountain air and the vibrant green of the Western Ghats. Our convoy was small: four bikes — three Triumphs and a trusty Himalayan — with one enthusiastic pillion adding to the group.

## An Early Start from Pune

Our day began at the first light of dawn with a 6:00 AM rendezvous at the Shell petrol pump near Nanded Phata in Pune. We topped off our fuel tanks in the cool morning air, geared up, and set off. If you are planning the same ride, an early departure is worth copying — it puts you on the ghat sections before the day warms up and leaves plenty of margin for the long return.

## The Departure Route: Sinhagad Road, Khadakwasla and Pabe Ghat

We charted a course from the familiar Sinhagad Road towards Khadakwasla, then wound up the picturesque Pabe Ghat. This stretch is particularly beautiful in the monsoon, with lush greenery enveloping the hills and mist dancing through the valleys. From Pabe Ghat we continued towards Velha, and then along the Gunjawani dam side towards our main destination, Madhe Ghat Waterfall.

En route we were treated to magnificent vistas of the historic Torna Fort standing proud in the distance, and the shimmering expanse of the Gunjawani Dam. We paused more than once to capture these moments with our cameras. The road through Pabe Ghat was in decent condition and made for a pleasant riding experience; for a more detailed account of that section, I have written separately about the Pabe Ghat route.

Our stomachs started rumbling around mid-morning, so we made a pit stop at the popular Rajtoran Misal for a hearty, delicious Maharashtrian breakfast.

## Madhe Ghat Waterfall: Two Hours at the Cascade

The Madhe Ghat Waterfall itself was a sight to behold. We spent nearly two hours there, mesmerised by the cascading water. We experienced it from two perspectives: very close to the base, and from a safer distance of about 100 metres at the viewpoint. From the viewpoint the waterfall looked absolutely stunning, with panoramic views of the surrounding Sahyadri hills stretching into the horizon.

For the adventurous there is a route to descend to the base of the waterfall. Standing directly beneath such a powerful cascade can be dangerous, although we did observe many thrill-seekers doing just that. One of our riders was carrying professional photography gear, including a drone, and the aerial shots were particularly amazing — a completely different perspective on the waterfall and its surroundings. After a round of snacks, we began the journey back home.

## The Return Route: Bhatghar Dam and Bhor Ghat

While the rest of the group planned to return the way we came, via Pabe Ghat, another fellow riding enthusiast and I decided to explore a different path: the route via Bhatghar Dam and the scenic Bhor Ghat road. This alternative proved to be even more captivating than Pabe Ghat during the monsoon. The entire landscape was painted in vibrant shades of green, a testament to the rejuvenating power of the rains.

Although longer, this route was undoubtedly worth the extra kilometres. We stopped countless times to soak in the beauty, and it offered us different perspectives of the imposing Torna and Rajgad forts, each view as awe-inspiring as the last.

The view of the Bhatghar Dam backwaters was truly breathtaking. The vast expanse of water, with gentle tides lapping the shores, resembled a serene sea. We took a leisurely break there, simply sitting by the water and enjoying the tranquillity. From Bhatghar we connected to NH4, where we opened up the throttle and bid farewell to the scenic ghats as we headed back towards Pune.

I reached home around 4:00 to 4:30 in the evening, the odometer clocking approximately 230 kilometres for the entire day's ride.

## Weather: A Surprisingly Dry Monsoon Day

Despite riding in the heart of the monsoon, we were surprisingly lucky with the weather. We encountered only a brief spell of rain lasting about ten minutes. For the majority of the ride the weather was humid and hot, which led us to shed our rain gear quite early on. The mountain ranges were shrouded in clouds that occasionally cut visibility, adding a mystical touch to the scenery.

## Riding Experience and Road Conditions

The ride to Madhe Ghat is generally considered fairly easy. While network connectivity was patchy in the ghat sections, road conditions were good for about 70–80% of the time. Riding my Scrambler, I had no complaints about the terrain.

I would recommend riding in groups, especially for amateur riders. Intermediate riders, however, can comfortably undertake this journey solo. Carry rain gear even if the forecast looks dry — the weather in the ghats can turn quickly — and keep some slack in your schedule for photo stops.

## Why This Ride Is Worth It

This monsoon motorcycle ride to Madhe Ghat Waterfall was a perfect blend of adventure, scenic beauty and camaraderie. The diverse routes in each direction — Pabe Ghat and Gunjawani on the way out, Bhatghar Dam and Bhor Ghat on the way back — the stunning views, and the majestic waterfall made for an unforgettable day trip. I highly recommend this ride to anyone looking to experience the magic of the Western Ghats during the monsoon season.

### Quick Facts

- **Destination:** Madhe Ghat Waterfall, via Pabe Ghat and Gunjawani Dam
- **Return route:** Bhatghar Dam and Bhor Ghat road, joining NH4
- **Duration:** 1 day, roughly 230 km
- **Start point:** Shell petrol pump, Nanded Phata, Pune, 6:00 AM
- **Breakfast stop:** Rajtoran Misal
- **Group:** 4 bikes (three Triumphs, one Himalayan) and 1 pillion
- **Weather:** brief ~10 minute rain spell, otherwise humid and hot; cloud cover over the ranges
- **Road condition:** good for about 70–80% of the route; patchy network in the ghats
- **Difficulty:** fairly easy; best done in a group for amateur riders
